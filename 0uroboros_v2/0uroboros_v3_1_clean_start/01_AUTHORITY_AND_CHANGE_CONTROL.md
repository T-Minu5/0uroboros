# Authority and Change Control

## Authority hierarchy
1. Mel's explicit current decisions
2. Canonical rules in this V3 package
3. Approved UX / product contract
4. First-party visual assets and concept art
5. Approved LookDev additions
6. Current repository implementation evidence
7. Mel-curated competitive / effects references
8. Historical implementation documents
9. External research

A lower-authority source never silently overrides a higher-authority source.

## Rule immutability
Implementation may iterate. **Approved game rules may not change without Mel.**

If a genuine unresolved gameplay rule blocks implementation, identify the exact missing decision, show only the minimum relevant context, and ask Mel. Do not invent a rule to keep coding.

## Approved UX decisions
Approved UX structure may be polished, spatially refined, animated, or materially upgraded. It may not be discarded because a specialist prefers a different information architecture.

## Creative references
Competitive games and visual references establish quality, interaction craft, motion principles, sequencing, tactility, hierarchy, and spectacle. They do not establish 0uroboros rules, palette, iconography, story, or identity.

## First-party assets
First-party card art, board concept art, icons, and intentional visual assets are product evidence. Do not replace them with generated substitutes when an intended first-party asset exists.

## Obsidian
The Obsidian vault is first-party **story / setting / plot / world lore**. It is not gameplay authority and is not automatically character-specific canon. Treat it as read-only unless Mel explicitly authorizes writes.

## New LookDev guidance
Mel may add more specific LookDev guidance over time. Put it in `10_LOOKDEV_ADDITIONS.md` or a clearly versioned successor. New approved LookDev guidance outranks generic creative suggestions but cannot override canonical game rules or locked UX requirements.
